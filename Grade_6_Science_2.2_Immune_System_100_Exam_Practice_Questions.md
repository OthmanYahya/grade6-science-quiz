**Important reading/marking notes grounded in the lesson:**
- The apple experiment is a **model** for unbroken skin, damaged skin, and cleaning a wound; the reported observation is what the slide answer sheet records, not a guarantee that every real apple will behave the same way.
- **Active immunity** is protection generated by the person's own immune response after infection or vaccination; **passive immunity** is protection from another source, including mother-to-baby transfer. The revision-sheet Venn diagram uses the simplified words “lifetime immunity” for active immunity; for scientific accuracy these questions use **can be long-lasting and forms immune memory**, rather than saying every vaccine/infection produces lifelong protection. The Chapter 2 review also notes the immune-memory distinction.
- The slides describe vaccines using **killed or weakened pathogens**. Questions follow that textbook explanation and focus on the underlying active-immunity concept.
- **Inflammation** is a protective response, but redness, warmth, swelling, and pain do not on their own identify the exact pathogen. The lesson describes fever as able to slow the growth of *some* pathogens, not as a cure.
