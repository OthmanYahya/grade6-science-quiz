# Grade 6 Science — Chapter 2, Section 2.1: The Nervous System

## Final-exam comprehension practice | 100 questions

**Level:** H · **Sources:** *Science Level H Ch 2.pptx* (Section 2.1, lesson slides 7–59 and relevant Chapter 2 review slides) and *2526–Level H–Science Revision Sheet–Final Term 1–Answer Key.docx* (Section 2.1, printed pages 27–33).

**Question breakdown:** 35 standard free-response + 15 *correct the mistake* free-response + 30 multiple-choice + 20 true/false = **100 questions**. Total free-response = **50**. Each question includes a **model answer**, an **if correct** explanation, and an **if incorrect** explanation in the answer key.

**Exam scope:** The nervous system’s function and divisions; central nervous system; peripheral nervous system (especially sensory vs. motor nerves); sense organs and stimuli; eyes and ears; reflexes; and the *measure reaction time* ruler-drop investigation. Questions emphasize explanation, comparison, prediction, sequencing, and exam-style scenarios rather than just recall. These are **original practice questions**, not claims about the actual exam.

**How to use:** Complete Parts A–D first, then use the separately numbered feedback in the Answer Key. For “correct the mistake,” rewrite the entire statement so it becomes scientifically accurate. For true/false, correct any false statement. For free response, equivalent scientifically correct wording should be accepted.

**Important science/marking notes:**
- **Sensory nerves:** receptors/sense organs **→ CNS**. **Motor nerves:** CNS **→ muscles/body organs**.
- A typical protective withdrawal reflex can be coordinated by the **spinal cord** without waiting for conscious thought. The brain may receive information about the event afterward; do not teach that every reflex is completely unrelated to the brain.
- In the *ruler-drop activity*, the measurement is the **centimeter mark caught**, not directly a time in seconds. With the same starting position, **shorter drop distance means a faster reaction**. Practice can improve results, but individual trials may still vary.
- The **pupil-size reflex** is included as an application because it appears in the slide deck’s Chapter 2 review (slides 173–175), not as a main subtopic of the initial Section 2.1 slides.
